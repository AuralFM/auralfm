Open most apps with no signal and you get a spinner. Open AuralFM and you get the whole world.

That is because the catalogue — every place, every station, every vibe — ships with the app. 38,337 stations across 12,579 places, packed small enough to live on your device. Search runs on your device. The globe spins on your device. Picking a vibe and watching the places light up happens on your device.

## Why build it this way

**Because it is faster.** Nothing has to be asked for. You type "jazz new orleans" and the answer is already there.

**Because it is fairer.** A search that happens on your device cannot be tuned by anyone else to favour whoever paid. There is no ranking server to lean on.

**Because connections are not equal.** A lot of the world listens on patchy, expensive mobile data. The less an app has to fetch just to show you where you are, the more of that data goes to the thing you came for: the sound.

## What still needs the network

The audio itself, of course — a live station is live. Headlines, the weather in the city you picked, the chat and the shortwave receivers are live too. Everything else is already home.

On an iPhone, adding AuralFM to your home screen keeps a copy of the app itself, so it opens like any other app even on a bad connection. The docs show you how.
