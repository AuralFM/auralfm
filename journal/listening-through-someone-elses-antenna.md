Shortwave is the strangest radio there is. The signal leaves a transmitter, bounces off the ionosphere and comes down thousands of kilometres away, with no company anywhere in the middle. To hear it you need an antenna, and most of us do not have one.

Some people do. Around the world, radio hobbyists run receivers called KiwiSDRs — a real antenna, in a real garden or on a real roof, connected to the internet so anyone can tune it from a browser. They list them on a public directory, openly, for anyone to use. The desktop app reads that same public list.

That is generous, and it is worth being clear about what it is not. **These operators are not AuralFM volunteers.** Most of them have never heard of us. They are sharing their equipment with the world, and we are one small part of the world passing through.

## How we try to be a good guest

- **We only connect when you click.** The app never scans receivers, never checks who is free, and never warms up a connection in the background.
- **One receiver at a time**, anywhere in the app.
- **We say who we are.** Every connection identifies itself as AuralFM, so an operator looking at their logs knows exactly what they are seeing.
- **We never retry a receiver that said no.** If it is full, we offer you a different one.
- **We give the slot back.** If you pause and walk away, the connection closes after ninety seconds instead of holding somebody's receiver for half an hour.

## If you run a receiver

If AuralFM shows up in your logs and you would rather it did not, email hello@auralfm.xyz with your host name. It is removed from the next build, no questions and no argument. You should never have to firewall us to make it stop.

And if you are happy to have listeners from all over the globe: thank you. You are the reason someone in Houston can hear what is crossing the sky over Finland tonight.
