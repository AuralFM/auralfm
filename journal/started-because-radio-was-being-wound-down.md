The name came years before the app. AuralFM started as an idea about *listening in* — not performing, not building an audience. Just hearing people.

## What I was running from

Somewhere along the way my playlists stopped sounding like anything. Every streaming service I used kept feeding me music from 2010 and older. I am in my thirties, and I was listening to the same handful of records on repeat, because that is what the algorithm had decided I was.

Radio in America was not the answer either. A few corporations own most of the dial, and the rest of it sounds like it was programmed in the same office.

## What I kept going back to

radio.garden, which is a beautiful piece of design, and which kept pulling me to Tokyo, Hawaii, Kenya, South Africa, Iceland — sounds and conversations I would never have been served, from a perspective I would never otherwise get.

The Tom Joyner Morning Show, which was my alarm clock for years, and the particular feeling of waking up to a human voice instead of a tone.

And the radio stations in Grand Theft Auto, honestly. Somebody underground, talking about god knows what, from somewhere strange. That is the register.

## Why now

Then Apple started winding radio out of Shortcuts and Apple Music, and the few easy ways I had to reach live stations started disappearing. If the big platforms were going to stop treating radio as something worth reaching, somebody had to build a door.

So: a globe. Every lit point is somewhere you can listen to. No chart, no feed, no account. Spin it, and go somewhere you have never heard.
