Radio, and the people on the other end of it

[MELTON BELL] 20-09-2026 · The network

Nothing in the middle

38,337 stream endpoints, 837 independently operated receivers and hundreds of relays, none of them ours. Every transmitter, every receiver, every relay belongs to somebody who is not us — and that is the point rather than an economy.

[MELTON BELL] 20-09-2026 · Shortwave

Borrowing an antenna

A KiwiSDR is one person’s receiver on one person’s bandwidth. The signal reaches it off the ionosphere with no company anywhere in the middle. That is the one part of this that is not a CDN.

[MELTON BELL] 20-09-2026 · Why it works offline

The catalogue lives on your machine

Hearing audio needs a connection, as it does in every radio app there is. What is unusual is that everything around the audio is already local: search, browse and the globe all work with the network unplugged.

[MELTON BELL] 20-09-2026 · Where it came from

Started because radio was being wound down

Inspired by radio.garden — the thing that kept pulling me back to Tokyo, Kenya, Iceland, South Africa — and begun when Apple started winding radio support out of Shortcuts and Apple Music.

What this is

A radio you look at. The globe is real — live cloud cover, the terminator where it actually falls, satellites where they actually are — and every lit point on it is somewhere you can listen to.

No account, nothing reported back, and no list anywhere of what you played.
