AuralFM — Privacy Policy

AuralFM plays internet radio. It comes as a Mac application and as a web version you can open in a browser or add to a phone's home screen. This policy covers both, and says clearly where the two differ — because on one point they do.

Short version: AuralFM has no accounts, no profiles and no tracking, and the app has no analytics. We do not collect what you listen to. But radio is the open internet. The station you play, and the services that draw the globe, see your IP address, the same way any website you visit does. Every one of them is named below, with what it sees.

What AuralFM stores

Nothing is stored anywhere but on the device in front of you. Where exactly depends on which version you are using.

The Mac application keeps these in your macOS Application Support folder:

FileContains
state.jsonFavourites, volume, last station, layer preferences
streams.jsonLast known working stream URL per station

You can delete any of it at any time. Removing the app removes it all.

The web version has no files of its own. Favourites, settings, the last station and your chat identity live in your browser's local storage for this site, and nowhere else. They do not follow you to another browser or another device, because there is no account to carry them. Clearing this site's data in your browser is a complete reset, and removing it from your home screen takes the same data with it.

If you add the web version to a home screen, it also keeps a copy of the app's own files — the page, the code, the globe's textures and the station list — so it opens without a network. That cache is the app itself, not anything about you.

What AuralFM does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-in, or profiles
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or usage tracking
  • No advertising, and no advertising or tracking SDKs of any kind
  • No collection of your name, email, location, contacts, or device identifiers (the one exception is on the website, not in the app: the waitlists, below)
  • No selling or sharing of data, because none is collected

Playing live radio and showing live conditions requires network requests. In the Mac application, every one of them goes directly from your computer to the service; none passes through any server we operate, and we never see them.

Two points worth stating plainly:

  1. Weather requests contain the coordinates of the city you clicked, chosen from a fixed public list — never your own location. AuralFM never requests or uses your device location, and never asks for the location permission.
  2. The globe opens on a city near you, worked out from your time zone. Your device's time zone is a name like Europe/Berlin, which the app matches against its own list of places so the first thing you see is somewhere plausible rather than the middle of the Pacific. This happens entirely on the device. The time zone is not sent anywhere, and it is a region, not a location — it cannot say which town you are in.
  3. Play counts are reported to radio-browser. Their documentation asks clients to do this so station popularity rankings stay meaningful. The report contains the station's identifier and nothing about you.

Each of these services has its own privacy policy, which governs what they do with a request once it reaches them.

What the web version routes differently

The web version is hosted on here.now, which serves the page and its files. Like any web host, it can see the requests that fetch them — your IP address, and which file you asked for. That is the cost of the app being a web page at all, and it applies from the moment you open it.

here.now also keeps page-view counts for the site, and we can read them: which page was opened, when, the referring site, and the country. They are counts of page loads, not a record of you — nothing in them says which station you played, what you favourited or what you wrote in chat, because none of that is ever sent. The desktop app has no equivalent: it has no analytics at all.

Headlines, and reading them, additionally pass through that host rather than going straight out, because a browser is not allowed to fetch them directly:

None of it is logged by us; we run no server and keep no logs. What here.now retains is governed by their policy, not ours.

The Mac application does not do this. It fetches the headlines and the articles directly, and nothing it does passes through a host of ours.

The waitlists (the website only)

This is the one place AuralFM asks you for anything, and it is on auralfm.xyz, not in the app. There are two lists: one for the desktop beta, and one for membership. If you join either, we keep:

  • your name and email address,
  • which computer you use (for the desktop beta) or which membership you are interested in,
  • anything you chose to write in the note.

It is stored with the company that hosts the website, as that site's own data. Only we can read it; it is not public. The host also sees your IP address when you send the form, and uses it to limit how many sign-ups one connection can send, which keeps spam out.

We use it for one thing: to email you about what you signed up for — an invite and a build for the beta, or membership opening. It is never sold, shared, or added to any other list. To come off either list, email hello@auralfm.xyz and we delete it.

Claiming a station (the website only)

People who run a station can claim its page with a form on docs.auralfm.xyz. It asks for the station, your name and email address, and whatever you choose to add: the station's website, a description, your links, a Nostr key and a Lightning address for tips. It is stored the same way as the waitlists: with the company that hosts the website, readable only by us, capped per connection.

We use your email to confirm the station is yours and for nothing else. The description, links and Lightning address are published on the station's page in the app, because that is what you sent them for; your name and email are never published. To change or remove any of it, send the form again or email hello@auralfm.xyz.

Shortwave receivers

The shortwave receivers on the globe are private machines — someone's radio and antenna, in someone's garden, connected at their own expense. Two things follow from that, and both are worth saying plainly.

A receiver you click sees your IP address and the frequency you tuned, in the same way a radio station's server sees your IP when you press play on a station. AuralFM connects only when you click a specific receiver, only to that one, and only one at a time. It never contacts a receiver in the background, never scans them to see who is free, and never retries one that refused — the list of who exists comes from a single aggregate file, not from asking every machine on the list.

The operator can see they have a listener. That is inherent to borrowing someone's antenna, and it is the same bargain the receiver's own web page offers.

Optional features that are off by default

Listener presence is off until you turn it on in Settings. It is the only thing in the app that sends anything, which is why it is off and why this section is the longest one here.

With it on, and only while you are playing something, AuralFM announces:

  • the station's name, and
  • the station's location, rounded to a grid cell of roughly 20 km.

It does not send your location. The coordinates announced are the transmitter's or the receiver's, not yours, and they are deliberately blunted: the cell is about 20 km across, so the announcement says "somewhere in this region" rather than naming a place.

There is no account, and no identifier that survives the app. A signing key is generated when AuralFM opens and thrown away when it closes. Nothing links one session to the next, which means nothing can accumulate into a profile — not here, and not at any relay.

The messages ask not to be stored. They are published as a type that relays are asked to forward and discard rather than retain. Most relays honour that. Nobody can guarantee that every relay does, which is the honest position, and it is why nothing in these messages is worth keeping in the first place.

Relays are chosen, not owned. AuralFM picks public relays near the place you are listening to, from a public directory plus a few well-known ones, and you can add your own in Settings — yours are tried first. A relay is asked only for messages in the cell you are listening to or looking at. No relay is told where you are.

Children

AuralFM is not directed at children, and it is for people aged 13 and over. The app collects no information from anyone. The waitlists are the one place anyone gives us a name or an email; if we learn that someone under 13 has joined one, we delete what they gave us.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Because the app is open to inspection, you can verify these claims in the source rather than take them on trust.

Live captions (off by default)

Removed 7 Aug 2026. Captions are no longer in the app. Enabling them
once downloaded about 1.3 GB of model weights that nothing ever cleaned up,
on a project whose whole argument is that it runs on hardware people already
own. The design, the measurements and what would have to change are in
CAPTIONS.md.

Switching captions on changes three things, and all three are worth stating plainly.

A one-time download from jsDelivr. The speech runtime — 11 MB of ONNX WebAssembly — is fetched the first time captions are enabled, not shipped inside the app. It used to be shipped, and carrying 11 MB in every download for a feature most people never switch on was the wrong trade. The cost of the change is honest and belongs here: enabling captions now tells one more third party (jsDelivr, a public npm mirror) that a request was made, once. Nothing is sent but the request.

Every one of those files is pinned to a SHA-256 recorded when the app was built, and checked before anything is written to disk or executed. A file that does not match is deleted and the feature refuses to start. This matters more than the download does: fetching executable WebAssembly from a CDN without verifying it would be handing a third party the ability to run code inside the app. The hashes come from the exact bytes the feature was tested against, so the CDN is trusted to serve the runtime and never to define what it is.

A one-time download from Hugging Face. The speech model's weights are fetched the first time captions are enabled — roughly 120 MB — and cached after that. This is the only request AuralFM makes to Hugging Face, it carries no information about you beyond the fact that a model was downloaded, and no audio ever leaves the machine. Whisper runs entirely in the app. There is no cloud speech API, because sending the audio somewhere would mean transmitting what you are listening to.

Streams load with crossOrigin. Web Audio cannot read a cross-origin stream — it returns silence — so captions require the station's server to permit it. This flag is applied only when captions are on; with the feature off, playback is byte-for-byte what it has always been. Where a station's server refuses, the app falls back to normal playback and says captions are unavailable there.
