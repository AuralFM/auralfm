# AuralFM — identity

The mark, the colour, and the icon. Written because two of these were decided in
passing and lived only as comments in `src/palettes.js` and `scripts/make-icons.js`,
which is not where anyone looks for a brand decision.

Status: 4 August 2026. Closes the colour and icon halves of #21.

---

## The mark

**The globe stays.** Two icons by *emilegraphics*, licensed royalty-free from the
Noun Project on 4 August 2026 — #1654366 filled, #1654367 outlined, $4.99 each.
Provenance and verification are in
[`assets/ATTRIBUTION.md`](../assets/ATTRIBUTION.md).

This was a deliberate call, made with the tradeoff on the table. **The licences
are non-exclusive**: the same globes are available to anyone for $4.99, so this
mark can be the app's icon and can never be something ownable or registrable. An
original mark is the only thing that fixes that.

The decision is to **not commission one yet**. The maintainer is a designer and
would rather pay a designer directly than generate a substitute, and neither is
worth doing while the app has one user and no release. Revisit when a registrable
identity actually buys something — which is roughly the same moment #15 resolves
toward money.

Recorded so the reasoning survives, and so nobody re-opens it as an oversight.

## The colour

**Onyx and bone.** Default palette `onyx`, and it is the brand rather than a
preference.

| Token | Value | |
|---|---|---|
| Accent (dark) | `#E9E2D4` | bone |
| Accent (light) | `#2A2622` | onyx |
| Station dots | `#EDE6D8` | |
| Receiver dots | `#C9A227` | brass |
| Atmosphere | `#9A9188` | |
| Stars | `#F5F0E4` | |

**Bone rather than white**, because white is what every system accent already
is; bone is warm and slightly soft, and it is what stops the app looking like it
borrowed its colours from the OS. **Onyx rather than black**, because real onyx
has depth — which is why the icon's field is a gradient and not a fill.

This supersedes the open question in #21, which framed the choice as *On Air*
green versus *Night Earth* blue. Neither won. Green was inherited from the idea
of a broadcast light and stopped describing the product once the app became a
dark planet; blue was the literal answer — the colour the globe already is — and
an identity that matches the artwork exactly has nothing left to contribute. Both
survive as selectable palettes, which is the right place for them.

The other six palettes recolour what surrounds the planet, never the planet:
the dominant surface is real satellite imagery and tinting it would destroy the
property the app is built on.

## The icon

Generated by `npm run icons` from the two SVGs — never hand-exported, because
for months nothing regenerated `build/icon.icns` and the packaged app shipped
whatever had been committed there.

**Two glyphs.** The outlined globe is beautiful at 128 and above and collapses
into a solid disc at 32, because its latitude lines merge once they are under a
pixel apart. The filled globe stays legible down to 16. Carrying different
artwork at different sizes is what `.icns` is for.

### Matched to the system, not to a guess

The icon is measured against `Music.app`, by reading alpha outward from the icon
body. Every number below is checked, not asserted:

| | macOS Music | AuralFM |
|---|---|---|
| Body, as % of canvas | 80.5% | **80.5%** |
| Corner inset on the diagonal | 6.8% of body | **6.6%** |
| Shadow alpha, 2px below / above / side | 60 / 8 / 33 | **57 / 7 / 30** |
| Shadow gone by | 24px | 24px |

Two things were wrong before this pass, and both are the kind that make an icon
read as a port rather than a Mac app:

- **The corner was a circular arc.** `border-radius` curves at a constant rate
  and stops dead where it meets the straight edge. Apple's corner is continuous —
  it eases in, so curvature never jumps. Now a sampled superellipse
  (`|x|⁵+|y|⁵=1`, 512 points), which brought the measured corner from 18.9% to
  6.6% against the system's 6.8%.
- **There was no shadow at all**, measured alpha 0 where the system has 33. The
  824/1024 body is not padding; macOS reserves that margin *for* the shadow, and
  leaving it empty wastes 20% of the canvas to draw nothing.

The shadow is one offset blur, not two. The first attempt stacked an ambient
under a key and measured 57 above the icon where the system has 8 — an ambient
term is what makes a shadow symmetrical, and a symmetrical shadow reads as a
sticker rather than an object resting on a surface. Parameters came from a sweep
over offset × blur × opacity scored as RMS error against the system profile at
five distances on three sides; it bottoms out at `0.012 / 0.014 / 0.32` with an
RMS of **2 out of 255**.

`drop-shadow`, not `box-shadow`: box-shadow is drawn from the border box and
would cast a rectangle's shadow from behind a squircle.
